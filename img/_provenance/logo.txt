Why the icons are redrawn rather than resized
  The source cannot be scaled down as-is, for three measured reasons:

  1. It is not square. Its opaque tile is 1172 x 1130 — 3.7% wider than tall.
     Any square export stretches the mark; any centre-crop to square cuts
     through the rounded corners.
  2. The navy is a diagonal gradient, #08397E at the upper left to #001A46 at
     the lower right, sampled in 61x61 patches. Below about 48px that reads as
     an uneven smudge rather than as depth.
  3. It carries an outer drop shadow. The file's alpha bbox is 1222 x 1208
     against the tile's 1172 x 1130, so the shadow is roughly 25px of
     half-transparent grey on every side. At 16px that is a dirty fringe and
     nothing else.

  So the build lifts the cream mark out as an anti-aliased mask (a luminance
  ramp, not a threshold, so the edges stay smooth) and redraws it on a square
  tile in one flat navy. The mark's geometry is the original's, untouched and
  untraced; only the parts that do not survive scaling are rebuilt.

Colours   Tile  #022A65 — the area-weighted mean of the source tile's 1,055,399
                navy pixels, so the flat fill sits where the gradient averaged.
          Mark  #FAF6EF — sampled inside the mark's left bar.
          Cream on navy measures 12.8:1, which is why the mark still reads at
          16px.

Sizes     16, 32, 48   favicon PNGs, and the three packed into favicon.ico.
          180          apple-touch-icon. Square with NO rounding: iOS applies
                       its own mask, and a pre-rounded icon puts transparent
                       corners inside Apple's rounding.
          192, 512     site.webmanifest, for Add to Home Screen.
          96           img/logo-96.png, the masthead lockup at 24px CSS.

  The mark is scaled UP as the icon gets smaller — 56% of the tile at 512px,
  74% at 16px. Its left bar is 7% of the mark's width, which at 16px is one
  device pixel; a single pixel of cream with padding around it is a smudge,
  so the padding gives way first.
