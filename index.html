A Day in Ancient Rome

Season 1

The clock, the bath, the rent — an ordinary day, by the numbers.

The shape of a Roman day
Rome ran on daylight, not clocks — and it made for a very strange kind of hour.
Where Rome slept
A million people — and almost none of them in the houses you're picturing.
What Rome ate
Most Romans could not cook at home. So the city cooked for them.
The water
Eleven aqueducts reached the city. Almost nobody had a tap.
The noise
Rome banned carts by day. So the city was loudest when you were trying to sleep.
Who did the work
The people who built and ran the city left almost nothing in writing.
How we know any of this
Almost everything in this season came from a very small slice of Rome.

The Last Pharaoh

Season 3

Three stories about Cleopatra, read against the people who wrote them down.

How did Cleopatra die?
The snake is in every painting of her, and nobody has ever found it.
The woman Rome had to invent
Almost everything ever written about Cleopatra was written by the side that beat her.
The tomb nobody has found
She asked to be buried beside Antony. Octavian agreed. Then the address went under water.

The Bible Never Said That

Season 4

A sin list, a lost golden box, and a woman the Church renamed.

The seven deadly sins
Not in the Bible. Assembled by monks over two centuries, then edited down by a pope.
The Ark of the Covenant
The Bible gives you the build spec, then stops mentioning it — and never says where it went.
Mary Magdalene
The most famous thing about her is something the Bible never says. It has a start date.

The awkward man

Every card tells you where it came from.

Not a bibliography at the back. Two independent sources minimum, on the card itself, one tap away.

“The seven deadly sins aren't biblical.”

  1. Evagrius Ponticus, Praktikos — written in the Egyptian desert in the 390s, the eight logismoi
  2. Gregory the Great, Moralia in Job, Book XXXI — begun in Constantinople c. 578, finished at Rome c. 595
  3. Morton W. Bloomfield, The Seven Deadly Sins — Michigan State University Press, 1952
