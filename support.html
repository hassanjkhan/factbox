Support

A person reads every message Usually a reply within a couple of days

Small site, small team. There is no ticket system and no bot. Write to hello@factbox.app about anything at all — a wrong date, a payment that went sideways, a story you want us to cover — and a human answers.

What is Factbox?

A history reader made for a phone. Season one is fifty-one stories, told across 450 cards, each one set on a painting or a photograph from the world's museum collections. A story takes about a minute and a half to two and a half minutes. There is nothing to install: it is a website, and it opens in whatever browser you are already holding.

What does it cost?

Two stories are free, with no payment and no sign-up, so you can see what it is before deciding. The rest of season one is a subscription to the site.

Several plans are offered, differing in billing period and price, and some include a free trial. The price, the billing period and the trial for the plan you pick are shown on the checkout page before you pay — we do not repeat the numbers here, because a page that repeats prices eventually contradicts the till. Payment is handled by Stripe on Stripe's own page; your card details never touch this site.

How do I cancel?

Open the billing portal and cancel it yourself. Enter the email you paid with, Stripe sends you a sign-in link, and cancelling takes one tap. You do not have to ask us and you do not have to wait for a reply. The same page lets you switch plans, update a card, and download past invoices.

If you would rather we did it, email hello@factbox.app and we will cancel it and confirm.

If you are on a free trial, cancelling before it ends means you are never charged. After that, cancelling stops the next charge and you keep reading for the period you have already paid for. The Terms set out the rest.

I paid, and now it is asking me to pay again

This is the most common message we get, and it is almost always the same cause. Access is remembered by the browser you paid in, because there is no account to log into. Open the site in a different browser — or on a different phone, or after clearing your browsing data — and it looks like a stranger.

The fix is your restore link. After you subscribe, the story shelf shows a box headed "Keep this link" with a long factbox.app address in it. Open that link on the new phone and it unlocks there.

  • Save it now if you have not. Email it to yourself. It is your only key.
  • Keep it private. Anyone who has the link has access — it works by possession, not by password.
  • Signing in does exactly this. Sign in with the same Google account, email or phone number you used, and your subscription is there — that is the whole point of having an account. The restore link below is only for anyone who subscribed before accounts existed.
  • Lost it? Write to us from the address you paid with, and we will find the payment and send you a working link.

A specific warning about Instagram and TikTok. Both open links inside their own in-app browser, which is not the same browser as Safari or Chrome and sometimes throws its storage away. If you subscribed inside one of those apps, get the restore link out into your real browser as soon as you can.

What happens to my reading progress?

It is stored in your browser and sent nowhere. That means the good part — nobody, us included, can see what you have read — and the awkward part:

  • Clearing your browsing data erases it, and we cannot restore it, because we never had a copy.
  • It does not follow you to another phone. The restore link carries your access across; it does not carry your progress.
  • In private browsing, and in a few in-app browsers, the browser refuses to save anything at all. Reading works fine; nothing is remembered afterwards. Where saving is unavailable the site says so rather than pretending the button worked.

The Privacy Policy lists every key the site writes, exactly as the code writes it.

Nothing is loading

If the shelf says it could not load the stories, that is a network problem between you and the site — reload the page. If a story opens but its picture does not, the text is still there and readable; the plate is the only thing missing.

Factbox needs JavaScript to assemble a story. If you block it, you will get a message saying so instead of a blank page.

The sound is not playing

A story can carry a quiet ambient sound bed behind it — a harbour, a gallery, a door. It is off unless you turn it on: one tap on, one tap off, and the site remembers your choice for next time. It is meant to sit under the reading rather than be noticed, so it is deliberately quiet, and quieter still if your system is set to reduce motion.

If you have switched it on and hear nothing, check the physical silent switch on the side of the phone, and the volume while the page is open. If your browser cannot play it at all, the control takes itself away rather than sitting there doing nothing.

Where does the artwork come from?

Every plate is from Wikimedia Commons — 361 of them across the season. Most are public domain. The rest are used under Creative Commons licences that require the photographer and the licence to be named and linked, and they are, on the card itself and again in full on the artwork credits page, which lists every plate with its source and its licence.

We download each file once and serve it from this domain rather than hotlinking Wikimedia, and we do not crop, recolour or filter what we serve.

Something in a card is wrong

Tell us, and please say which story and which card. This is the most useful message you can send: getting the history right is the whole reason the thing exists, so a correction is a gift rather than a complaint.

If your source disagrees with ours, send yours and we will look at both. Where a card rests on a specific document or a disputed reading, the card says so — but not every card carries a citation, and we would rather be told than assumed correct.

Can I suggest a story?

Please do. The catalogue is written by hand, so suggestions genuinely shape what gets made next. Tell us the subject and, if you have one, the thing about it that surprised you — that is usually where a good card starts.

What do you know about me?

Effectively nothing. If you signed up, your email address and your answers to the onboarding questions are stored in your own browser — there is no server behind the sign-up form, so we never receive them. The one exception is that your email is put into the Stripe checkout page so you do not type it twice. Your reading never leaves your browser at all. Payment is Stripe's, so Stripe holds your billing details and we do not. We measure which stories people finish and how long each card holds you, through PostHog, so that the next stories are better than the last. It stores a random id in your browser and there is a one-tap off switch on the Privacy page. The Privacy Policy is the full account, key by key.

Accessibility

If your system is set to reduce motion, the site respects it: animation and transitions are switched off and the pages simply hold still. Every control is built to a 44-pixel minimum touch target. If something is hard to use with an assistive technology, tell us what and on which page — we would rather fix it than have you work around it.
