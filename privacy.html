Factbox for iOS

Privacy Policy

Factbox keeps everything on your phone. There is no account, no server of ours, and nothing to log in to — so there is very little for this document to be about.

Effective 1 September 2026 Version 0.1.0 Applies to: Factbox for iOS
Draft. This is written to describe what the app genuinely does today, but it has not been reviewed by a lawyer, and the contact address below is a placeholder. Both need settling before Factbox goes beyond TestFlight.

The short version

  • We don't ask you to make an account, and we never ask for your email.
  • Your reading progress, streak and saved cards live in the app's own storage on your device. We can't see them.
  • There is no analytics, no tracking, no advertising identifier, and no third-party code in the app.
  • The app makes no network requests of its own. Nothing is uploaded anywhere.
  • Delete the app and everything it held goes with it.
  • The one exception is feedback you choose to send us — and you see exactly what's attached before it leaves.

01 Who this is from

Factbox is a history app for iPhone, made by an independent developer. In this policy, “we” means that developer and “the app” means Factbox for iOS. This policy covers the app only. It doesn't cover Apple's services, which have their own privacy policy.

03 What stays on your phone

The app stores a small amount of information locally so it can work at all: the name you gave (if you gave one), which days you've read and how far into each, your streak and reading history, cards you've saved, your chosen reminder time, and whether reminders are on.

This sits in the app's own storage on your device, in the ordinary way iOS apps store settings. We have no way to read it — there is no server to send it to, and the app makes no network requests of its own.

It also means the data isn't backed up to us. If you delete the app, all of it goes. We can't restore it, because we never had it.

04 Feedback you send us

The app has a feedback form under You → Help & feedback. It doesn't upload anything. It opens your own mail app with a message drafted for you, which you can read, edit, or throw away before sending.

Along with what you write, that draft includes some technical detail so we can make sense of a bug report:

  • The app version and build number
  • Your iOS version and device model — for example “iPhone 16 Pro”
  • Which season and day you're on, and how many days you've finished
  • Whether reminders are switched on

All of it is shown to you in the app under “What gets sent” before you send anything, and it's all visible in the draft email itself. If you'd rather not send some of it, delete it from the message — it's your email.

If your device has no mail account set up, the app copies the same text to your clipboard instead so you can paste it wherever you like. Nothing is sent in that case either.

When you do email us, we keep the message so we can reply and fix the thing you told us about. Ask us to delete it and we will.

05 TestFlight

While Factbox is in beta, it's distributed through Apple's TestFlight. If you send feedback or a screenshot through TestFlight itself, or if the app crashes and you allow Apple to share diagnostics, Apple passes that to us — including your TestFlight email address and basic device information.

That's Apple's channel rather than ours, and it's governed by Apple's privacy policy and your TestFlight settings. We only ever see it because you chose to submit something.

06 Subscriptions and payments

Any subscription is bought through Apple's In-App Purchase. Apple handles the payment and tells the app only whether a subscription is active. We never see your card, your billing address, or your Apple Account.

Managing or cancelling a subscription happens in your iOS Settings, not in the app.

07 Notifications

The daily reminder is scheduled by your phone, on your phone. There is no push server, and no notification token is sent anywhere. The app works out which day you haven't read and asks iOS to show a reminder at the time you chose.

You can turn reminders off in the app under You → Notifications, or revoke permission entirely in iOS Settings.

08 Third parties

There are none inside the app. Factbox ships with no analytics SDK, no advertising SDK, no crash reporter of ours, and no third-party libraries at all. Nothing about your reading is shared with anyone, because nothing about your reading leaves your phone.

We don't sell or share personal information, and we never have. There is nothing to sell.

09 Children

Factbox isn't aimed at children under 13 and we don't knowingly collect anything from them. Given that the app collects no personal information from anyone, a child using it would leave no data with us either. If you believe a child has sent us something through feedback, email us and we'll delete it.

10 Your rights

Depending on where you live — the UK and EU under GDPR, California under the CCPA, and elsewhere — you have rights to see, correct, export or delete the personal information a company holds about you.

For Factbox those rights are unusually simple to exercise, because we hold nothing by default:

  • To see or export your data: it's on your phone, in the app. There's no copy with us.
  • To delete it: delete the app, or use You → Reset onboarding to wipe your profile in place.
  • If you've emailed us feedback: that message is the one thing we hold. Ask and we'll delete it and confirm.

You never need to ask permission to use the app anonymously — that's the only way it works.

11 Security

The strongest thing we can say about security is that we've reduced the surface: there's no account to breach, no database of readers to leak, and no credentials to steal. Data on your device is protected by iOS and your passcode.

Email is not encrypted end to end, so please don't put anything sensitive in a feedback message.

12 Changes to this policy

If the app starts doing something this document doesn't describe — syncing your progress across devices, say, which would need a server — we'll update this page before that ships, and change the effective date at the top. Substantial changes will be called out in the app's release notes rather than quietly edited in.
